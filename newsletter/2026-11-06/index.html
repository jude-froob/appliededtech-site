🇦🇺 Australian Spotlight

Education ministers agree to restrict generative AI in primary schools and assessments

ABC News (Conor Duffy)  ·  5 Nov 2026

Federal, state and territory education ministers agreed to set up an Australian Schools Digital Detox Taskforce and to restrict generative AI in primary schools and secondary school assessments. Year 5 NAPLAN writing tests will also return to paper.

Why it matters: What "restrict" will mean in practice is still to come, so it is a good time to check how your school handles AI in primary classes and assessments, and to watch for guidance from your system.

🌏 Worldwide

Google will turn off Search and Gemini for K-12 student accounts by default

CNBC (Isabel O'Brien)  ·  3 Nov 2026

Google says Gemini, Gemini Notebook and Search will be off by default for K-12 student accounts, and schools can turn each back on. Critics including Sen. Bernie Sanders and NYC Mayor Zohran Mamdani objected to reports that schools rejecting AI entirely would lose Search, and Google's education vice president Chris Phillips said "Search and AI are deeply integrated."

Why it matters: Search and AI are now closely bundled, so find out which Google settings your administrators have chosen for student accounts and what that means for younger year levels.

Seattle schools ban Gemini and Minecraft after parents rally for an AI pause

KIRO 7  ·  5 Nov 2026

Seattle Public Schools announced it will ban Gemini AI and Minecraft and block YouTube for younger students, after parents and students rallied for a pause on classroom AI. From January the district will stop giving each K-2 student an individual iPad, though shared devices will stay in classrooms.

Why it matters: Parent concern about young children and screens is now shaping district decisions, so be ready to explain which devices and AI tools your class uses, and why.

Parents want AI taught with strong safeguards, but many teachers lack training

EdSurge (April Rubin)  ·  3 Nov 2026

A National Parents Union survey of 1,528 US K-12 parents found 77% want public schools to teach students to use AI tools effectively, and 71% support banning student use of AI chatbots throughout the school day. In an Educators for Excellence teacher survey, 43% said they had received no AI training at all.

Why it matters: Parents want both AI literacy and limits, so clear communication about when AI is used in your classroom, and training that keeps pace, will matter.

University of Minnesota experts warn careless AI use can disrupt learning, especially in the early years

MPR News (Elizabeth Shockman)  ·  28 Oct 2026

An expert-consensus report found a shortage of high-quality research on how AI affects learning, and broad agreement that some uses could cause "severe and one-sided" disruption to key parts of learning. Experts urged extra caution with young students, and lead author Panayiota Kendeou said students should first work independently so "their ideas and their agency is preserved."

Why it matters: If AI is used with students at all, build in independent thinking first, and ask what evidence supports any tool before adopting it.
