Teachers drop take-home essays as AI changes how students write

The San Francisco Standard  ·  21 Oct 2026

Reporter Ezra Wallach follows teachers such as Eliza Russo at Lincoln High, who stopped setting take-home essays after AI-written papers appeared in September 2024 and now has students write by hand in class. The article also cites a study of about 27,000 Chinese students followed for 30 months, where homework scores rose 18% after AI use began but closed-book exam scores fell 20% within six months.

Why it matters: In-class handwriting protects the thinking but, as Russo notes, limits longer arguments, so plan where drafting and revision get taught as well as where they get checked.

Nearly 40% of Finnish 11 to 17-year-olds can't reliably tell AI images from real ones

Yle  ·  22 Oct 2026

A survey of more than 1,000 Finnish 11 to 17-year-olds, run by E2 Research for Digirauha and the Itla Children's Foundation, found nearly half use AI every day and nearly 80 percent use it in some form. The most common uses were looking up information and getting help with schoolwork, and more than half said they trust what AI tells them.

Why it matters: Students are already using AI as an information source, so checking claims and spotting synthetic media is worth teaching explicitly rather than assuming they can do it.

Colorado students are sharing explicit AI images of classmates

The Denver Gazette  ·  21 Oct 2026

Safe2Tell data shows at least two cases this year of Colorado students creating and sharing explicit AI images of classmates, though the program does not track AI so the true number is unknown. The article cites National Center for Missing & Exploited Children figures rising from 4,700 in 2023 to 440,000 in the first six months of 2025, and a Common Sense Media survey in which only 13% of young people said they would feel comfortable talking to a teacher or counsellor.

Why it matters: Check that your behaviour and wellbeing policies name AI-generated images and that students know who they can safely tell; Common Sense Media recommends strong school policies and curricula plus a safe space to talk.

A Charlotte-area school district pauses most AI tools on student devices

WCNC (via Yahoo News)  ·  21 Oct 2026

From 21 October, Clover School District blocks public AI tools on student devices, calling it a temporary pause while it sets clearer expectations for responsible use and protects student information. SchoolAI is the one tool still allowed, for teachers and middle and high school students, while elementary students have no access.

Why it matters: A pause with one vetted tool and innovation coaches for teachers is a middle path between a ban and a free-for-all, and a model for trialling a single approved tool before widening access.

England teachers' AI use: planning and parent emails up, marking and data analysis far below forecasts

Teacher Tapp  ·  6 Oct 2026

Teacher Tapp compared 2023 predictions with 2026 reported use. Primary teachers' AI use for lesson planning rose from a predicted 39% to 55%, and primary senior leaders' use for parent communications from about 28% to 56%, while secondary teachers' use for marking was about 7% against 31% predicted.

Why it matters: Teachers are using AI most for planning and communication, not marking, a useful reality check when choosing what to focus on in staff professional learning.
