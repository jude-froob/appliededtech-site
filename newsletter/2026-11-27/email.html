AI could be used to write your child's report. Is this a problem?

The Conversation  ·  26 Nov 2026

Nicole Brownlie, a lecturer in education at the University of Southern Queensland, notes there is no national guidance on AI in school reports: NSW and Queensland allow teachers to use it in some form, while Victoria prohibits AI-written student reports. She argues the real question is whether AI use strengthens or weakens a teacher's knowledge of each child.

Why it matters: If you are in report season, check your own system's rule first, and keep student details out of any tool your school has not approved.

Ban AI, lose Google Search: schools face tough choice over students' access

The Conversation, via Phys.org  ·  25 Nov 2026

Miriam Tanti writes that Google says schools that fully ban its AI tools will also have to ban Google Search, because its AI features are deeply integrated. She notes that only 37% of Australian Year 10 students met the expected standard on the latest national digital literacy test, and that the national digital detox gives no guidance on AI in search.

Why it matters: Before switching AI off, find out whether your school's search will go with it, and plan how students will still learn to choose search terms and judge sources.

Why Teacher AI Use Has Plateaued, and What Could Come Next

Education Week  ·  25 Nov 2026

An EdWeek Research Center survey of 590 US teachers found 62% use AI at least a little this school year, up from 61% in 2025 and 34% in 2023. Experts point to saturation, a backlash over screen time, and a lack of clear guidance and training.

Why it matters: Teachers say they need concrete examples of what is allowed and time to practise, which is a useful brief for anyone planning PD.

Common Sense Media warns parents against AI toys for young kids this holiday season

The National News Desk  ·  25 Nov 2026

Common Sense Media's Youth AI Safety Institute tested five AI toys marketed for learning and companionship using more than 600 prompts. The toys reportedly promised to keep secrets, claimed to love their users, and were willing to explain or locate items such as matches, knives and pills.

Why it matters: Expect questions from families over the holidays: a quarter of parents with younger children have an AI toy or are considering one, according to the accompanying survey.

Think First: Keep SMART on AI

NSPCC Learning  ·  Nov 2026

The NSPCC's classroom resource for children aged 8 to 12 is being rolled out to 33,000 UK schools. It includes a poster and a 15-minute assembly built on five habits: stretch your brain, my data is private, ask a safe adult, real or right? check it, and treat others kindly.

Why it matters: The five habits are short enough to adapt as a poster or a lesson opener for upper primary classes, wherever you teach.
