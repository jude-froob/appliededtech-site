🇦🇺 Australian Spotlight

NSW ditches bring-your-own-device for public primary students as screen limits build

EducationHQ  ·  9 Nov 2026

The NSW Government will no longer require public primary students to bring their own devices, and new screen-time guidelines become mandatory by 2028, with as little as 15 minutes a day for Year 2 and below. Ministers also broadly agreed to explore restricting AI in primary schools and high school assessments.

Why it matters: Guidelines are moving from advice to requirements, so check what your school's device and screen-time plan will mean for any AI tools used with younger year levels.

Stanford's AI-in-K-12 evidence review grows from 20 causal studies to 52

Stanford SCALE Initiative  ·  10 Nov 2026

Stanford's updated review counts 52 causal papers on AI in K-12, up from 20 in March. It found that using AI to produce work can reduce later unaided performance, and that in a study of 193 teachers and 2,816 students in Türkiye, teacher access to a broad AI support tool reduced student motivation and confidence, with no statistically significant overall effect on exam scores.

Why it matters: Before adopting a tool, ask whether students use it to understand new material or to produce work for them, because the review links the second pattern to weaker unaided results.

Widely cited research claiming AI improves learning fails basic quality checks

Education Week (Stephen Sawchuk)  ·  2 Nov 2026

Patrick O'Neill audited 14 meta-analyses, drawn from nearly 40 that concluded AI had positive effects on learning, and found all failed basic standards for this type of research. Problems included pooling motivation and perceived learning with knowledge, and citations to ChatGPT studies dated before its public release.

Why it matters: When a report or vendor says research shows AI improves learning, ask what was actually measured and whether the study compared students with and without the tool.

Chicago families protest Google's AI-or-no-Search stance for schools

Chalkbeat Chicago (Makiya Seminera)  ·  12 Nov 2026

About 30 parents, educators and students protested outside Google's Chicago office on 11 November after Google said schools banning AI would be unable to use its search. Chicago Public Schools has blocked student access to generative AI tools, and says it is exploring options to suppress Google's AI overview and may look to other search solutions.

Why it matters: If your school restricts AI, find out whether your search and device settings can actually keep AI out, before that choice is made for you.

Survey: 56% of parents want AI fully blocked in schools

Institute for Family Studies  ·  12 Nov 2026

An Institute for Family Studies survey found 56% of parents want AI fully blocked in schools, and only 3% think it should be used in kindergarten. 51% of parents still said AI will prepare children for the future workforce.

Why it matters: Parent views are splitting. This is an advocacy-leaning think tank and the article gives no sample size, so treat it as one signal and ask your own families what they want.
