🇦🇺 Australian Spotlight
 

AERO urges sparing use of AI, and none in the first three years of primary

ABC News  ·  14 Oct 2026

The Australian Education Research Organisation says heavy early AI use can lead to "cognitive outsourcing", where students let the tool do the thinking and miss out on building knowledge and skills. CEO Dr Jenny Donovan advises avoiding AI entirely in the first three years of primary school, then using it carefully alongside explicit instruction.

Why it matters: It is a clear steer from Australia's education research body for primary staff rooms: ask what each AI use is doing for student thinking, and set clear schoolwide expectations about when independent work is required.

Tasmanian students petition against a school AI chatbot trial

Togatus  ·  30 Sep 2026

Tasmania's Department for Children and Young People is trialling a generative AI chatbot, similar to Microsoft Copilot, in select classrooms. Students at Hellyer College have gathered over 250 signatures on a petition citing concerns about brain development, critical thinking, the environment and their futures.

Why it matters: Student voice is now part of the AI rollout conversation, and the article notes schools, principals and teachers would carry responsibility for misuse, so it is worth knowing what your own system's tool does and does not guarantee.

🌏 Worldwide
 

Nearly two in three teachers using AI say it frees up time

Gallup  ·  14 Oct 2026

A Walton Family Foundation-Gallup survey of 1,952 US K-12 educators found 59% used AI tools in 2025-26, for tasks such as lesson prep, materials, grading and admin. Among users, 64% said it freed up time for the parts of teaching they value most, while 29% saw no effect and 7% said it cost them time.

Why it matters: It is a balanced look at the workload claim: most users report a gain, but more than one in three do not, so target AI at the admin-heavy tasks that really drain your week.

Report calls for federal funding of AI-in-schools research

Education Week  ·  15 Oct 2026

The Alliance for Learning Innovation points to a Stanford SCALE Initiative analysis of more than 800 academic papers on AI in K-12, of which only 20 produced strong causal evidence. It urges the US Congress and administration to fund research into safe, effective classroom AI.

Why it matters: When a vendor says a tool "works", the fair question is what evidence sits behind it, and this number is a useful one to bring to a purchasing discussion.

More than half of New York school districts have no formal AI policy

New York Focus  ·  13 Oct 2026

New York Focus built a crawler to check about 678 districts' websites and school board platforms, then contacted roughly 400 districts where it found nothing. More than half lack a board-adopted AI policy, and 39 of the 70-plus that confirmed having none said they plan to write one this school year.

Why it matters: Where there is no policy, decisions fall to individual teachers, so it is a prompt to check that your own school's AI expectations are written down and shared with staff.

Most research on generative AI and learning leaves out children

MentalHealthDaily (Frontiers in Psychology review)  ·  10 Oct 2026

A mini review of 46 studies found 35 sampled university students and only 8 included anyone under 18, while 26 were cross-sectional surveys. The authors propose "calibrated delegation": teaching children when AI can support their thinking and when to work through a problem themselves.

Why it matters: It backs AERO's caution: much of what is claimed about AI and learning comes from adults, so be wary of applying it to primary classrooms. The authors argue how AI is used matters more than how often.

How to use AI with English learners without encouraging cognitive offloading

Education Week (opinion)  ·  15 Oct 2026

In Larry Ferlazzo's Classroom Q&A, two teachers share routines such as a handwritten journal warm-up with laptops away and a Socratic AI tutor that asks questions rather than giving answers. Students also keep an AI Co-Authorship Journal reflecting on when they chose AI and when they chose analog methods.

Why it matters: These are concrete, low-cost routines you can try this week, and they fit AERO's advice to scaffold learning rather than replace student thinking.
