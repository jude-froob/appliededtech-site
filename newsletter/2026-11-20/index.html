More than half of NSW teachers back pausing take-home assessments, survey finds

The Educator K/12 (Jhoanna Hines)  ·  18 Nov 2026

A Gradeo survey of 200 NSW schools, cited in The Australian, found about 91% of teachers say detecting AI use is no longer enough and assessment itself needs redesigning, and about 53% back a temporary ban on take-home tasks. Years 11 and 12 are now limited to one take-home assessment task worth no more than 7.5% of a student's HSC mark.

Why it matters: If you set tasks that students complete at home, think about where you could see some of the thinking happen, as Concord High School did by adding a spoken assessment after final work far exceeded students' usual performance.

🌏 Worldwide

Teachers say AI professional development needs hands-on practice, not another demo

Education Week (Lauraine Langreo)  ·  16 Nov 2026

An EdWeek Research Center survey of 852 educators found about two-thirds have attended at least one AI PD session, up 11 percentage points from 56% last year. Teachers say sessions need hands-on practice and time to talk with peers, and should cover drawbacks such as AI-enabled cheating, which many sessions skip.

Why it matters: If you plan AI PD, give people a realistic sample task to work on and leave room for the awkward questions about cheating and critical thinking.

Teacher education programs are teaching AI, but unevenly

Education Week (Alyson Klein)  ·  16 Nov 2026

An EdWeek Research Center survey of 268 US teacher educators found 60% say individual instructors decide whether to include an AI focus, and most programs offer no standalone AI course. About a third say faculty or instructors don't support teaching it, and 57% cite AI's rapid development as a hurdle.

Why it matters: New graduates will arrive with very different AI preparation, so ask early career colleagues what they have covered rather than assuming.

South Korea to inspect glasses at every exam after an AI cheating confession

Al Jazeera (Shola Lawal)  ·  17 Nov 2026

A top scorer on a national exam for aspiring tax accountants confessed to photographing questions with a miniature camera and sending them to an AI tool. The education minister announced glasses inspections at every test session, and the ministry wants to extend exam bans for offenders from one year to three.

Why it matters: Wearable cameras and phones are now an exam-security question, so check that your school's assessment rules mention smart glasses and watches.

Only 42% of Alpha School students met its 'twice as fast' learning goal

Fortune (Muskaan Arshad)  ·  14 Nov 2026

Fortune reports internal data, first covered by The New York Times, showing 42% of students at the AI-driven private school network, which charges up to $75,000 a year, met its goal of learning twice as fast in both math and reading. About half of K-8 students at its private campuses missed the math target.

Why it matters: When a vendor or school promises students will learn twice as fast, ask who measured it, against what, and how many students actually met the target.
